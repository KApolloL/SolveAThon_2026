    Where does this list come from, and can I trust it?

      Glossary: what the words mean
      Best match
      How many words a grant's title and summary share with your agency's plan goals. It is a keyword score, not a judgment of quality. Compare scores only inside one agency's list.
      Eligible (your agency can apply)
      The listing's list of who can apply, or a quoted line in the announcement, says state agencies are allowed.
      Eligibility unclear
      The applicant list is unclear. The announcement's own words are shown under the title; open the grant announcement (the official notice on Grants.gov, linked on the title) before spending time.
      Who signs
      Who has to approve an application: you can start now, your manager or decision maker must sign, or leadership must sign. Shown only when the announcement makes it clear.
      Closing soon
      14 days or fewer left to apply.
      Coming up
      Forecasts: the funder says it plans to post this. You cannot apply yet, but you can plan.
      Rolling
      No close date is listed. Open the grant announcement (the official notice on Grants.gov) to see how long it stays open.
      Pursue, Maybe, Skip
      Your own marks, saved in this browser. Pursue puts the grant in My grants and opens a plan. Each mark (agency, grant and mark only; no name or email) is also sent to the project team to measure the pilot.
      Tier 1, 2, 3
      Who has to sign, as in the qualifier tool. Tier 1: you can start. Tier 2: your manager signs (matching money). Tier 3: leadership signs (matching money on an award of $1M or more).
      Impact (≈)
      A 0 to 100 score: 60% how well the grant fits your agency's plan, 40% how big one award can be ($10K scores 0, $100M or more scores 100). Strategic plan fit only when no amount is listed. Our estimate; it never changes the ranking.
      Past wins
      From federal award records. ★★★ your agency has won this program before; ★★ another NC group has; ★ your agency has won other grants from the same federal department. No stars means "New to NC": no NC win on record, which is not a bad sign.
      Odds (≈)
      A rough read of competition from the listing: how many awards will be made, who else is allowed to apply, and whether NC has won this program before. Good, Fair, Long shot, or Unknown when the listing says nothing. It never changes the ranking.
      Effort (≈)
      Easy, Medium or Hard from who signs and whether matching money is needed. The weeks shown (2, 4, 8) are our rough guess, not a measurement.
      Payoff (≈)
      The most one award can be, divided by those rough weeks of work. Shown only when the listing says your agency can apply. Use it to compare grants, not as a promise of money.
      Refine (optional)

      The list works with this closed. Everything here changes what is shown or how your own view is ordered; the stored keyword fit scores are never changed.
