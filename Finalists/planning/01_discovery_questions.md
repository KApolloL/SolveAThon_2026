# Client discovery questions (Fri 6:30 to 7:30 PM workshop)

The workshop is the only live access to NC government staff. Ask clear questions to drive the conversation; do not let data drive it. Write down their words, not our interpretation.

## Roles (agree before the workshop)
- **Asker:** leads questions, keeps to the priority list. Not the note-taker.
- **Note-taker:** records answers word for word in the sheet below. Does not ask unless the asker invites it.
- **Follow-up asker:** listens for vague answers and probes ("can you give an example?").
- **Timekeeper:** keeps to the plan; flags when the top questions are not yet answered.
- **Kent:** listens for what the data must contain; asks the technical questions last.

## Revised question list (Fri Nov 6, 6:45 pm, after the kickoff talks)
Sources: Kent's kickoff notes (vault `Finals/Untitled.md`): David Yokum's school bus example and "users don't know what they want"; Warren Watts' (Deloitte) question types. Ask in this order. Stars mark must-get answers.

### 1. The user (who, and what they can handle)
1. * Who would use this day to day, and who else has a stake (supervisor, agency head, OSBM)? Are they the same person?
2. * How do they find federal opportunities today? Walk us through the last time: what did you open, what did you decide, how long did it take?
3. How comfortable are they with new tools? Would they need training, and who would give it?
4. Is the process the same in every agency and division, or different? What levels sign off at each office?

### 2. The problem (anchor on the school bus case)
5. * Walk us through the school bus funding that NC missed. Where exactly did it break down: not knowing it existed, not knowing it fit, or not gathering the pieces in time?
6. * How would you know how many opportunities are missed today? Is anyone counting?
7. How does someone decide an opportunity fits? Which documents do they check (strategic plan, statute, program priorities, eligibility)?
8. What happens after a match is found? What has to be collected before an application, and by when?

### 3. Success and trust
9. * What would a good result look like on Monday morning? How many items could you act on?
10. * What would make you distrust or stop using it (one bad match, a black-box score, jargon)?
11. Which is worse for you: a bad match shown, or a good one missed?
12. What criteria would make OSBM call this a success after a pilot?

### 4. Scope, features and vision
13. * Should this cover every agency at once, or start with one? If one, which, and why?
14. What features matter most: ranked list, summaries, deadline alerts, feedback buttons? What is out of scope or already handled by someone else?
15. If this worked perfectly, what would change for the agencies and for the people the grants serve? (They may not know what they want: listen for a bigger vision than the brief.)
16. Would a coordinator spend 5 minutes ranking 10 sample grants at the start so the tool learns their priorities? What would make them skip it? Who decides relevance: an individual or a supervisor?
17. When you reject a grant, what is the reason (deadline, match requirement, mission, size)?

### 5. Constraints and fit with the organization
18. * What tools are used now, and should this be a tailored build or something that sits on an off-the-shelf product (COTS)?
19. What restrictions apply: clearances, data rules, business rules, procurement, accessibility?
20. Budget and staffing: what could a pilot cost, who would run it, and who maintains feedback data?
21. Calendar: fiscal-year, grant-cycle or reporting deadlines we should design around? (Ask; do not assume the fiscal year.)
22. Data: is Grants.gov enough, or do you also need SAM.gov? Can we use the strategic plans as posted on the OSBM site? Which agencies have weak or outdated plans?

### 6. Kent, last (technical)
23. Any Azure services, rules or limits we should know about?
24. Who on your side would take this over after Sunday, and what would they need in the README?

### For the Sunday explanation (listen for these; the judges want them)
How it was built, why it was built, how someone continues building it, and who else it would affect beyond the direct user.

## Probes (use when an answer is vague)
- "Can you give me an example from last week?"
- "What would you do differently if that were true?"
- "How would you measure that?"
- "Who would disagree with that?"
- "What is the first thing you would check to see if this result is wrong?"
- Close by stating back: "So the user is X, and they must decide Y, and a good answer looks like Z. Is that right?"

## Do not
- Do not pitch our idea or ask leading questions.
- Do not ask for personal or confidential data. If anyone offers it, decline.
- Do not leave without the user, the decision, and what a good answer looks like in the client's own words.
