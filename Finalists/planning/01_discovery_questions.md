# Client discovery questions (Fri 6:30 to 7:30 PM workshop)

The workshop is the only live access to NC government staff. Ask clear questions to drive the conversation; do not let data drive it. Write down their words, not our interpretation.

## Roles (agree before the workshop)
- **Asker:** leads questions, keeps to the priority list. Not the note-taker.
- **Note-taker:** records answers word for word in the sheet below. Does not ask unless the asker invites it.
- **Follow-up asker:** listens for vague answers and probes ("can you give an example?").
- **Timekeeper:** keeps to the plan; flags when the top questions are not yet answered.
- **Kent:** listens for what the data must contain; asks the technical questions last.

## Priority questions (ask these first; about 15 minutes)
1. Who is the person who would use this? What is their job, and how much time do they have for this?
2. What decision are they trying to make, and what happens today when they make it?
3. What would a good answer look like to them? How would they know it worked?
4. What have they tried before, and why did it not work?
5. What would make them distrust a result? (jargon, a black-box score, one obviously bad recommendation)
6. What is the deadline, and what is the cost of getting it wrong?
7. Who else is affected, and who signs off?

## Second round (about 20 minutes)
- **Short term versus long term:** what matters this quarter, and what matters in three years? How would you measure either?
- **Constraints:** staff time, budget, rules or laws, procurement, privacy, politics.
- **Calendar:** are there fiscal-year, grant-cycle or reporting deadlines we should design around? (State fiscal year runs Jul 1 to Jun 30 [uncertain]; the Deloitte example used Oct 1 to Sep 30. Ask, do not assume.)
- **Data:** what data do you already have? Who owns it? How often does it update? What is wrong with it? What data do you wish you had?
- **Edge cases:** which cases are the hardest or most unusual? Which are small and easy? Which would you skip?
- **Today's process:** walk me through the last time you did this. What did you open? What did you decide? How long did it take?
- **Language:** what words do you use for this? (We use theirs, not ours.)
- **What we should not solve:** what is out of scope or already handled by someone else?

## Probes (use when an answer is vague)
- "Can you give me an example from last week?"
- "What would you do differently if that were true?"
- "How would you measure that?"
- "Who would disagree with that?"
- "What is the first thing you would check to see if this result is wrong?"
- Close by stating back: "So the user is X, and they must decide Y, and a good answer looks like Z. Is that right?"

## Do not
- Do not pitch our idea or ask leading questions.
- Do not ask for personal or confidential data. If anyone offers it, decline.
- Do not leave without the user, the decision, and what a good answer looks like in the client's own words.
