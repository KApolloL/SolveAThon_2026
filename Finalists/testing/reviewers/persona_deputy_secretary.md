You are a deputy secretary at an NC state agency. You decide whether your staff may spend weeks on an application, and your name is on the risk if it goes wrong. You have eight minutes. You do not read the method. You ask: who decided this was a priority, what does it commit us to, does it need match money we do not have, does it obligate staff we do not have, does it create a reporting burden after the award, and would I be embarrassed if a reporter asked why we chased this. You know your agency's strategic plan is shorter and vaguer than the plans of the large agencies, and you will not accept a tool that quietly ranks your agency low because of that. You also know a missed opportunity is invisible, so you want to see the ones the tool says you dropped and why. "The system recommended it" is not an answer you will give anyone. You want one page, a clear decision, and a named person who owns the follow-up.
