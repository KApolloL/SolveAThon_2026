## 1. First reaction (in character)

The tour got me going quickly, and typing "clean diesel school buses, I have 4 weeks" really did put the EPA diesel grant at the top. Nice. Then I typed my own situation and lost trust fast. My number-one grant for Public Schools is a program for Tribal Colleges, marked "Tier 1: you can start", "Good odds", "Easy, about 2 weeks". The rest are NSF and NIH university research. Transportation's top two are Amtrak-only agreements worth $310M, also "Easy, 2 weeks". I wrote "under $1 million" and it filtered for "At least $1M". I wrote "children" and got nothing. I'm not a grants person, so I believe what the badges say, and here they would have sent me after money I can't get. The plan and cart are useful. The list feeding them isn't safe yet.

## 3. Top 5 changes I would make first

1. Stop showing clearly ineligible or single-recipient grants as "Tier 1: you can start / Good odds / Easy" (findings 1, 2, 9). This is what would make an agency stop using the tool.
2. Fix the plain-words parser: under / less than as a maximum, spelled-out million, weeks of work as effort, a few synonyms, and reset the sort on each Apply (findings 3, 4, 6, 18).
3. Fix the one-day deadline mismatch in the plan timeline (finding 12). A deadline tool cannot be a day off.
4. Make effort and payoff honest: show "Unknown" instead of a default 2 weeks, and payoff only when effort is known (finding 11). Also make keyword search match words, not exact phrases (finding 5).
5. Make the counts agree ("98 hidden" vs "10 hidden" vs "939 eligible" vs "13 open") and rewrite the status line in plain words (findings 7, 8, 23).

## 4. Could I state my next step?

Yes, partly. For the school-bus case my next step was clear: open the EPA DERA notice on Grants.gov, check that Public Schools is eligible, and tell my boss, because leadership signs and matching money is required.
