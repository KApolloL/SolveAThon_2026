- **Problem in one sentence:** NC agencies miss or find late federal funding opportunities because finding and matching them to agency priorities is manual, uneven and nobody owns it; OSBM wants an AI-assisted Federal Funds Matchmaker that surfaces promising leads early while humans make the final call. [`docs/QUEST_BRIEF.md`]
- **Worked example the sponsors gave:** the federal school bus funding. NC missed the first-year deadline because it was not noticed in time. Application window "about one month" is from discovery notes and is not verified. [`planning/07_discovery_findings.md`]
- **Sponsor and users:** Sponsor is OSBM (State Budget and Management). In the discovery room: a grants lead at Natural and Cultural Resources (one person writes grants and screens Grants.gov constantly), Public Instruction (nobody in charge of scanning; each person scans a little), State IT Office of AI (a project manager and an architect), OSBM. No "grant explorer" job exists. Many users are not grant experts. OSBM will have no dedicated team; likely a cross-agency partnership. [`planning/07_discovery_findings.md`]
- **Primary user (working assumption):** an agency staffer who triages federal opportunities for one agency, often on top of another job. [assumed until Kent confirms]
- **Supervisor / decision maker:** [assumed] the staffer's division head or deputy secretary decides if the staffer may spend time; an OSBM budget director decides whether a pilot gets money. Names are not recorded and must not be.
- **Decision the user makes, and the action our result should trigger:** which opportunities are worth pursuing for the agency and whether to act before the deadline. The tool surfaces a short list with a plain reason; a human decides. Hard rule from the brief: the system supports judgment and never replaces it.
- **What the sponsors said that changed the plan:** time is the key constraint and more questions for users is bad; rank by payoff versus effort, not fit alone; the tool, not the person, sorts eligibility; waiting minutes for an answer fails; do not send sensitive data to an arbitrary LLM; Grants.gov alone is not comprehensive; a tool that also drafts the proposal would be top tier (stretch only). [`planning/07_discovery_findings.md`]
- **What we are building (plan, not necessarily built; check `handoff.md` for status):** one scored table of every opportunity against every agency, with an agency picker as the main screen. Fit is scored first by keyword similarity, with Azure embeddings as a swap. Clearly ineligible items are hidden with a visible count and a toggle; unclear eligibility reads "check eligibility". The flag is the top 10 per agency per window (window chosen in the UI, default 2 weeks). Short reasons quote the plan and the notice, and a script checks each quote appears in its source. The qualifier's signature-tier idea is a second axis. Daily Azure job refreshes data; a "closing soon" panel; an optional good/bad feedback button. Runs on Azure Container Apps with Blob storage. [`planning/08_product_decisions.md`]
- **Where the outputs are (planned):** `data/processed/opportunities.csv` (built), the ranking file and `payload.json` (not yet), `data/manual/criteria.csv` (not yet written), `deliverables/slide_brief.md` (not yet), `src/docs/fact_sheet.md` (empty), `src/docs/qa_cheatsheet.md` (template). Marco's Azure proof app is live per `handoff.md`.
- **Criteria:** `data/manual/criteria.csv` does not exist yet. Stated criteria so far are fit to agency strategic goals, deadline, award size, eligibility, and effort or burden, each to be tied to a data field with a sponsor quote. Work from `planning/08_product_decisions.md` until the table exists.
- **Data used:** Grants.gov (extract plus live search), Simpler.Grants.gov (cross-check only; added 0 rows), Federal Register notices, USAspending context. 6,523 opportunity rows: 882 posted, 588 forecasted, 4,947 closed since 2024-11-06, 104 notices. 34 NC agency strategic plans from OSBM, 158 goal rows. Not pulled: SAM.gov catalog (key allows too few calls), USDOT page (403 to scripts). Known problems: 70 posted items with close dates after 2030 (some 2099); 723 of 1,472 open items have no award ceiling; plan text partly OCR (DHHS especially); stale plans for Auditor, Elections, Wildlife Resources, Commerce (Commerce is FY21-23); Commerce, DOT and Agriculture goal labels are thin. [`handoff.md`]
- **Test pool and success test:** three agencies (Public Schools, Environmental Quality, Health and Human Services), 20 items each, two labelers per item, labels relevant / maybe / not relevant. Pass bar: at least 70% recall in the agency's top 10. 12 of 20 pool items were picked by keywords, so recall can be overstated. Labels are "team-labelled", never ground truth. With 20 items one item moves recall 5 to 10 points. Backtest curve at 2, 4 and 6 weeks over closed items, with "all catches" and "in-time catches" (at least 21 days left; 21 is a team assumption, not a sponsor number). [`drafts/claude/eval/`, `planning/08_product_decisions.md`]
- **Weaknesses we already know about (named weakness is recall versus shortlist length):** small hand-labelled pool; keyword fit is a weak proxy for agency fit; stale or thin plans for some agencies; missing sources (SAM.gov, USDOT, foundations, earmarks); "missed" only means the tool would have flagged it, not that the agency never saw it; "NC has won before" is a context column, never proof; notice recall is [uncertain]; Azure OpenAI availability and privacy-from-training are [uncertain]; the repo is private, so judges' access is open.
